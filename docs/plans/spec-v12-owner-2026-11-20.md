# Owner specification v12 (received 2026-11-20, browser-audit UI fixes and the dragon's forced move)

Two owner texts received together: the approval to fix the five player-experience defects the 26-scenario browser audit found on `keep-engine`, and the owner's decision on the dragon's forced movement and trapped heroes (the edge case reported in the inventory of [`unresolved-gameplay-2026-11-20.md`](unresolved-gameplay-2026-11-20.md) after spec v11 decision 6). Where this file disagrees with an earlier spec or register ruling, this file wins. Owner text is quoted verbatim in the blocks; do not reinterpret it.

This is not an approval of the catalogue or the game: every card stays `draft` (162 entries) and the implementation is not approved. The UI pass changes presentation only (no rule, card, dice, turn-order or hidden-information change). The second text keeps the existing engine behaviour and asks only for verification and regression tests (`tests/keep/spec-v12.test.js`); it is recorded as an owner ruling in plan section 13 (D19) and the inventory.

> APPROVED — Fix all five browser-discovered UI problems on keep-engine.
> The 26-scenario browser audit has identified five player-experience defects. Please fix all five now.
> Primary objective: Every significant game event should clearly communicate what happened, why it happened, and what the player needs to do next.
> Preserve all approved game mechanics, dice probabilities, card data, turn order and hidden-information rules.
> 1. Automatic card effects — FIX
> Cards that resolve automatically must still be visible to the player. Examples include: Poisonous mist; Explosion; Chest contents; Room cards following a door; Other automatic damage or status effects.
> Requirements: Display the drawn card's title, event description and result. Show the actual dice rolls when applicable. Clearly identify damage, HP changes, skipped turns and acquired items. Keep the result visible until the player acknowledges it. Do not advance past an important result before the player can read it. Preserve proper card order and existing resolution mechanics.
> Use a compact, mobile-friendly result interface rather than temporary toast notifications.
> 2. Dice result explanations — FIX
> Every dice result needs a plain-language explanation. For example, if Bones rolls an even number, explain that the skeleton remains inactive. Do not show only a number without explaining its consequence. Clearly distinguish: Success; Failure; Damage; Death; No effect; Additional action required.
> 3. Giant Spider interface — FIX
> Correct the spider's combat result wording. When the matching number kills the spider, explicitly say the spider is defeated. Do not use "nothing happens" for a successful kill. Break long instructions into readable steps. Keep the number picker and confirmation controls visible and accessible. Test portrait, landscape and desktop layouts.
> Do not alter the actual spider combat mechanics.
> 4. Dragon awakening announcement — FIX
> The dragon waking must be a significant, clearly communicated event. When the dragon awakens: Display an unmistakable announcement that the dragon is awake. Explain that affected heroes lose their Dragon's Lair treasure. Show fire damage and resulting HP changes. Explain the forced-exit consequences. Show whether the dragon remains awake or resets after the chamber empties.
> Do not send players directly to an empty treasure screen without explaining why their treasure disappeared.
> Preserve multiplayer hidden information and the existing dragon mechanics.
> 5. Reliable screen refresh — FIX
> The visible game state should not depend exclusively on animation-frame execution. After acknowledging a result: Update the displayed game state reliably. Ensure the board, HP, inventory, prompts and turn indicator remain synchronized. Handle backgrounded or temporarily hidden browser tabs correctly. Avoid stale screens or requiring a manual refresh. Do not introduce artificial delays or unnecessary animations.
> Use the application's existing state-management architecture rather than introducing unrelated systems.
> 6. Verification
> After fixing these issues: 1. Run the complete test suite and content checker. 2. Repeat the affected browser scenarios. 3. Test at phone portrait, phone landscape and desktop widths. 4. Include multiplayer handoffs and hidden-information checks. 5. Check that the result screens are usable with keyboard navigation and accessible labels. 6. Confirm that no new regressions or unintended gameplay changes occurred.
> Report each fix separately, including the tests and browser observations supporting it.
> 7. Keep unrelated gameplay decisions separate
> Do not change the following during this UI pass: Closed doors during forced movement (decision 8). Dragon movement through walls. Flask availability during a forced skip. Treasure coin quantities. Monster combat card source verification. Hero names or biographies.
> The dragon-forced movement onto a well through a wall is a potentially significant edge case. Please document a reproducible scenario and explain how the current engine handles it, but do not change the movement rules without approval.
> Do not mark the 162 draft entries approved as part of this work.
> Keep everything on keep-engine. Do not merge, push or deploy.

> Owner decision — Dragon forced movement and trapped heroes
> Keep the existing dragon-forced movement behavior.
> Section 10.1 allows movement to any adjacent labyrinth tile without explicitly requiring a free passage. Therefore, a hero may be forced onto an adjacent tile even if a wall separates it from the Dragon's Lair.
> If that results in a hero becoming trapped, that is an acceptable gameplay outcome. Drakborgen is intentionally dangerous, and heroes are not guaranteed an escape route.
> Do not introduce automatic rescue, teleportation, extra exits, or special exceptions to prevent heroes from becoming trapped.
> Just verify that trapped heroes follow their applicable tile rules, can still take any genuinely legal actions, and eventually lose when sunset arrives if they cannot escape.
> Preserve the current mechanics and add regression tests if necessary.
> Keep everything on keep-engine. No merge, push, or deployment.
